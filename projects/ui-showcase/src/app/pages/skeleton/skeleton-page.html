size × shape

text is a line at the height of that size's text, circle an avatar, rect a media block. text and rect fill their container's width; the matrix cells are 12rem wide for that reason.

animation

pulse fades the bones in and out, wave sweeps a highlight across them, none holds still. Under prefers-reduced-motion both animations stop, whatever the input says.

lines

lines stacks bars with the theme's spacing between them, and past one line the last is shorter — the silhouette a real paragraph leaves.

@for (count of lineCounts; track count) {
[lines]="{{ count }}"
}

width, height and rounded

width and height take any CSS length, to match the thing that will replace the bone. A circle given only a width stays a circle. height is ignored on text, whose line height comes from size. [rounded]="false" squares the corners for content that has square ones.

width="8rem"
circle, width="4rem"
height="6rem" [rounded]="false"

composed — a card that loads

A screen loads a layout, not a shape: an avatar, a title, a paragraph, a picture. Built from bones the size of what replaces them, the card keeps its height while it loads — 308px loading against 307px loaded here — so nothing below it moves when the content lands. The one part that needed matching by hand was the paragraph: it wraps to three lines, so its bone has [lines]="3"; with two it was 21px short. A bone's line height comes from its size, not from your font, so count the lines and measure. The card reloads for two seconds on demand.

host attributes

Read from the live DOM. A bone is aria-hidden; give ariaLabel to the one that stands for the whole loading area, and it becomes role="status" with that name. That makes the loading area findable while reading — it does not announce anything when it appears, because a live region speaks its text and a bone has none. What tells a screen reader the area is changing is aria-busy on the container, as the card above sets, and what tells it the content has arrived is the content.

skeleton or spinner

A skeleton when the layout of what is coming is known — a list, a card, a profile — so the page does not jump when it lands. A spinner when it is not, or when the wait belongs to an action rather than to content: a button that is saving, a search that is running.
