size × state

The calendar button is not optional — it is the one affordance that says this field is more than a text box, so it is always there and it is what openCalendarLabel names. The clear button is the opposite: clearable is false by default, as on every control except gog-multiselect.

the trailing slot, and the clear button

[clearable]="true" puts a second button beside the calendar one, and unlike gog-autocomplete it watches the value rather than the text: a half-typed date that has not parsed yet leaves it hidden, because there is nothing committed to clear.

fullWidth, in a 20rem container

Full width of its container by default, like every other field. With [fullWidth]="false" the field falls back to the browser's intrinsic width for a text input — this control has no minWidth input, because there is no selected label for it to collapse onto.

a field with no visible label

ariaLabel names a field that has no label, and the panel takes the same name — with neither it falls back to the calendar button's own label. placeholder is empty by default, which is why stating the format in it is worth doing.

range

Two months side by side is what makes a range picker usable. min, max, and disabledDates

min and max bound the range; disabledDates takes a predicate rather than a list, because an array cannot express "weekends" or "public holidays" without the consumer materialising every date first. A disabled day is a disabled <button>: it is announced, and neither the pointer nor Enter takes it. defaultMonth decides which month opens when there is nothing selected yet.

A field with a time does not close when a day is picked — the clock is below the grid and would be unreachable if it did, so the panel waits for a press outside or Esc. That is the one behaviour difference the input makes, and it is why format gains HH:mm on its own when showTime is on. minuteStep moves the minute spinner in larger jumps; hourFormat="12" adds the AM/PM toggle.

value: {{ show(withTime(), 'dd.MM.yyyy HH:mm') }}

A pattern rather than a locale format, so what the field prints can always be read back. Three rules are worth knowing, and each is visible in the fields below. A draft that does not parse leaves the value alone rather than clearing it — otherwise every keystroke of a date fires a null at an attached form — and blur snaps the text back to whatever the value actually is. An impossible date is rejected rather than rolled over: 31.02.2026 stays as text and the value does not become 3 March. And a range field is read-only whatever allowTextInput says, because there is no pattern for two dates and a dash.

locale and firstDayOfWeek

Open each and compare the header and the weekday row. The first day of the week follows the locale unless firstDayOfWeek overrides it — 0 is Sunday. The date format is deliberately not derived from the locale: it is the pattern in format, so a German app that wants dd.MM.yyyy gets it without asking, and one that wants yyyy-MM-dd says so.

Both fields below sit in a box with overflow: hidden and are pinned to dropdownDirection="down", so which way they open does not depend on where the page happens to be scrolled: open both and only the appended one survives the box. No field and no panel — [inline]="true" renders literally gog-calendar, which is why that component is exported on its own. Every input that describes the grid still applies; the ones that describe the field (label, clearable, floatLabel, appendToBody) have nothing to act on.

valueChange and gogDateSelect

The two outputs are not the same event. valueChange fires for every change, including the first press of a range; gogDateSelect fires only when the selection is complete — a day in single mode, both ends in range mode — which is what the field uses to decide when to close. Only the calendar has it: bind it on gog-datepicker and there is no such output.

the two footer buttons

Deliberately two controls, not one. Today selects today's date, and the view follows because the selection is what the calendar opens on. This month only moves the view back and leaves the selection alone, for browsing far from today without committing to anything. One button doing both read as "take me back" to one person and "set it to today" to another, and no wording fixed that. Where min puts today out of range, Today is disabled rather than silently doing nothing.

keyboard

From the field: ↓ or Enter opens the panel, Esc closes it — from anywhere inside it, the calendar button and the day grid included, and the field takes the focus back. Inside the grid the arrows move a day at a time and a week at a time, Home and End go to the ends of the week, PageUp and PageDown change the month — with Shift, the year — and Enter or Space picks. The 42 day cells are one tab stop, not 42: a roving tabindex keeps exactly one of them tabbable, the arrows move it, and the focus ring goes with it.

Two of those sentences were only true from 21.15.0, and this page is why. Opening with the calendar button left focus on the button, where Esc was not handled at all — the dialog could be opened with the keyboard and not dismissed with it. And the arrows moved the roving tabindex without moving the focus, so the ring stood still while the highlight walked away and Enter committed a day the ring was not on.

panel and grid

The panel exists only while it is open, so the first two lines read "no element matches" until the field beside them is opened. The grid is a real role="grid" of gridcells — a month is two-dimensional, and that is what tells assistive tech the arrows mean "next day" and "next week". Today's cell carries aria-current="date", a selected one sits in a cell with aria-selected="true", and a day outside min/max or excluded by disabledDates is a disabled button.
